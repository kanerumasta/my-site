Hi,

You ask an AI to clean up a function.

It renames three variables, invents a package that does not exist, and confidently calls the result production-ready.

Five minutes later, your news feed says AI could end humanity.

That is quite a change of mood for one coffee break.

[![Large AI letters shattering a fragile Earth](https://www.macibale.site/images/ai-shatters-earth-hero.png)](https://www.macibale.site/blog/ai-could-kill-us-all-so-wheres-the-threat-model?utm_source=buttondown&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=ai_could_kill_us_all)

A recent warning from people working close to frontier AI went viral. The reactions were predictable: panic on one side, ridicule on the other.

I do not think either response helps.

The most important word in the headline is **could**. For the worst outcome to become possible, several things would need to come together:

**Capability + harmful behavior + autonomy + access + failed oversight**

That chain is frightening, but it is also useful. Every link gives us somewhere to intervene. We can restrict access, limit autonomy, test behavior, require human approval, and make sure somebody can actually stop the system.

My new article looks past the scary percentage and asks a more practical question: what would have to go wrong, and what could prevent it?
